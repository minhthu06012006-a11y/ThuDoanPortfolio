Lý Kéo Chài

A digital illustration inspired by Lễ Cá Ông — the sacred Whale Worship Festival of Vietnamese coastal communities. A tribute to the fishermen who brave the sea and the spiritual bond between humans and the ocean.

Watch full process ↗
01 — Cultural Context

Lễ Cá Ông — Nghinh Ông

Lễ Cá Ông (Whale Worship Festival), also known as Lễ Nghinh Ông, is one of the most sacred festivals of Vietnamese coastal communities — particularly in the South. Rooted in over 200 years of tradition, it is held annually between the 14th and 17th of the 8th lunar month in provinces like Bình Thuận, Bà Rịa–Vũng Tàu, and Cà Mau.

Fishermen believe the whale (Cá Ông — "Lord Fish") is a divine protector sent by the Goddess of the Sea to guide lost sailors home. When a whale washes ashore, it is given a full funeral and buried with deep respect. After three years, its bones are ceremonially enshrined in a local đình (communal house).

The festival features grand processions of decorated fishing boats, ritual offerings, traditional music (Hát Bội), and the haunting folk song Lý Kéo Chài — a rowing chant that fishermen sing in rhythm with pulling their nets, that became the soul of this artwork.

🐳
Divine Guardian
Whales are called "Cá Ông" — Lord Fish — believed to be sacred protectors of fishermen at sea
🎵
Lý Kéo Chài
A traditional folk chant sung by fishermen while pulling nets — its rhythm carries the labor and soul of the sea
🛶
200+ Years
One of the oldest continuously practiced maritime festivals in Southern Vietnam, listed as national intangible cultural heritage
🌊
Community Bond
The festival unites entire coastal villages — a rare moment when the sea is not a source of danger but of celebration
02 — Visual Direction & Moodboard

The visual language draws from three sources: the vivid palette of Vietnamese lacquer painting (sơn mài), the dramatic compositions of traditional woodblock prints (tranh Đông Hồ), and the raw energy of contemporary digital illustration. The goal was to feel ancient but alive.

Lacquer Red
The ceremonial red of festival lanterns, the fire of ritual incense, the pulse of a living tradition
Deep Sea Navy
The unfathomable depth of the South China Sea at dusk — vast, mysterious, both threat and home
Festival Gold
The glow of offerings, the shimmer of fish scales, the warmth of torchlight on weathered skin
Coastal Sage
Mossy fishing nets, weathered bamboo poles, the patient green of mangroves at the water's edge

Câu Chuyện Tình Yêu

A wordless love story told through sequential illustration — two figures, a shared world, and the quiet moments that make up the space between falling and staying in love.

Watch full timelapse ↗
01 — Concept & Inspiration

Love as a Visual Language

This project explores the idea that love doesn't need words — it lives in glances, in hands reaching for each other, in the way two people share the same light. The illustrations tell a story without dialogue, relying entirely on composition, color, and body language to carry emotion.

Inspired by the visual poetry of East Asian romance manhwa and the quiet intimacy of Studio Ghibli's background art, the style blends soft pastel washes with precise line work — warm and dreamlike, but grounded in genuine human feeling.

Each scene in the sequence is designed to stand alone as a complete moment, while contributing to a larger emotional arc — from the nervous first glance, through shared adventures, to the comfortable silence of a love that has settled in.

💭
Wordless Story
Every emotion is carried by pose, color, and composition — no text, no captions, just visual rhythm
🎨
Soft Anime Style
Pastel-forward palette with precise ink lines — influenced by shoujo manga and healing illustration aesthetics
✨
Sequential Art
Each frame is both a standalone piece and part of a larger emotional journey — like pages in a visual novel
🌸
Character Design
The two characters were designed to visually complement each other — contrasting silhouettes that feel incomplete apart
02 — Visual Direction & Moodboard

The palette was built around warmth and softness — colors that feel like late afternoon light, like the flutter before a confession, like the exhale of a long-held feeling finally released.

Soft Lavender
